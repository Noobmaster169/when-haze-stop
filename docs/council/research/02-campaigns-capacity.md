# 02 - Historical campaigns, institutions and Malaysia's real capacity

Research date: 2026-10-10. Scope: what Malaysia can actually send to fight Indonesian fires, what has
worked before, who decides, and what it costs. Written as reference for a product that ranks which
Indonesian hotspots a small Malaysian assistance force should suppress first to cut haze in Kuala Lumpur.

Labels used throughout:

- **[V]** verified: stated by an official body or by two or more independent outlets.
- **[R]** reported: one outlet, or outlets that disagree; usable but flag it.
- **[A]** assumption: not public; a value proposed for the product, with what it rests on.
- **[K]** background knowledge from before this research, not re-checked in this session.

Research limits: the shared web-search budget ran out before the last round of queries (the Malaysian
haze committee membership, the ASEAN ministers' meeting, and whether the Malaysian team has landed in
Kalimantan). Those gaps are marked. Several Malay Mail, Berita Harian and aerialfiremag pages returned
HTTP 403. Where that happened, their content is cited from search-result summaries and marked [R].

### 1.2 The package as announced [V]

- **1 Bombardier CL-415MP** amphibious water bomber from APMM (the Malaysian Maritime Enforcement Agency, MMEA).
- **3 helicopters.** No outlet names the type or the owning agency.
- **52 officers and personnel** from NADMA, APMM and TUDM (RMAF). JBPM (Bomba) is a coordinating agency, not a listed contributor of personnel.
- **Destination: Kalimantan.** This is Prabowo's wording, and every Indonesian and Malaysian report repeats it.
- Zahid framed it as "not meant to interfere in Indonesia's affairs" and as the first step of a longer prevention plan.

As of 7 to 9 October, I found no report that the team had deployed. Assume it has not [R]. A later
search for arrival news could not be run because the search budget was exhausted.

### 1.3 Where it is burning

Hotspot counts depend on the method: Kemenhut "high confidence" SiPongi counts are 5 to 25 times
smaller than BMKG all-confidence counts. Never mix the two in one series.

- **Season to date (ASMC, VIIRS daytime high confidence, 1 Jan to 27 Aug):** 15,652 hotspots, of which Kalimantan 12,212 (78%) and Sumatra 3,440. Kalimantan rose from 1,339 in July to 9,849 in August. Even part-year, this is Kalimantan's third-highest total of the decade (2019: 19,681; 2023: 14,219). [V] The Star, 29 Aug 2026 (above).
- **1 Sep (Kemenhut, high confidence):** national 1,370. West Kalimantan 570, Central Kalimantan 488, South Kalimantan 64, South Sumatra 19, Riau 0, Jambi 0. Air fleet 53 (34 water bombing, 19 patrol and weather modification). Six priority provinces: Central Kalimantan 569, South Sumatra 145, West Kalimantan 69, South Kalimantan 28, Riau 4, Jambi 0. Suppression is focused on Central Kalimantan. 61 aircraft on standby (44 water bombing, 17 patrol and weather modification). [V] "Titik Panas Karhutla Nasional Melonjak Lagi", Republika, 7 Oct 2026, https://news.republika.co.id/berita/tmiboa393/titik-panas-karhutla-nasional-melonjak-lagi

### 1.4 What Indonesia itself has mobilised (2026) [V]

- 20 Sep: 64 aircraft doing water bombing since August (55 BNPB, 9 TNI) and **54,394 joint personnel** (TNI, Polri, Forestry, BNPB, BPBD, Manggala Agni, volunteers, communities). Source: Cabinet Secretary Teddy Indra Wijaya. "Gov't Mobilizes 64 Aircraft, 54,394 Personnel...", Setkab, 20 Sep 2026, https://setkab.go.id/en/govt-mobilizes-64-aircraft-54394-personnel-to-handle-forest-land-fires/
- BNPB operates at least 52 helicopters for karhutla this year, paid after the operation and audited by BPKP. [R] Katadata, https://katadata.co.id/finansial/makro/6a96bf94ddd6d/bnpb-ajukan-anggaran-tambahan-ke-kemenkeu-untuk-penanganan-bencana
- Enforcement: 219 criminal investigations, 162 suspects and 95 companies under investigation (17 Sep). Prabowo calls slash-and-burn "ecological terrorism". [R] Wikipedia, "2026 Southeast Asian haze", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Southeast_Asian_haze

**Scale check.** The Malaysian package is 4 aircraft against Indonesia's 61 to 64 (about 6%) and
52 people against 54,394 (about 0.1%). Its value therefore depends almost entirely on where it is
sent. That is the product's reason to exist.

### 1.5 Other foreign help in 2026

- **Japan:** 3 JGSDF CH-47 Chinooks and about 300 to 350 personnel on JS Kunisaki, docked at Mempawah, West Kalimantan. This is the JSDF's first overseas firefighting mission, planned for 12 to 19 Sep. Smoke grounded them repeatedly: visibility fell below 3 km and flights were cancelled. One report says only one of the three could operate in Ketapang. Operations were rescheduled to resume on 24 Sep. [V] "Three Japanese helicopters join West Kalimantan wildfire fight", ANTARA, https://en.antaranews.com/news/430849/three-japanese-helicopters-join-west-kalimantan-wildfire-fight ; The Star/AFP, 17 Sep 2026 (above).
- **Singapore:** Minister Grace Fu says Singapore "stands ready to assist" (10 Oct). No package has been announced. [R]

**Lesson for the product.** Smoke grounds helicopters. A target that sits inside a dense smoke plume
may be unflyable. The product should down-weight targets where visibility is forecast below about 3 km.

### 1.6 Malaysian air quality, 2026 [V]

- Sarawak was hit first (August). Serian reached 521 (4 Sep). On 1 Sep: Serian 388, Kuching 363, Sarikei 352, Samarahan 333, Sibu 329, Sri Aman 319. All 591 schools in 10 Sarawak districts closed on 20 Aug.
- Peninsula, 6 Oct at 6 pm: Batu Pahat 196, Nilai 174, Seremban 171, Putrajaya 166, Cheras 163, Johan Setia 163, Shah Alam 161. On 8 Oct, Segamat reached 190, with 38 areas unhealthy. On 9 Oct: Shah Alam 176, Batu Muda (KL) 172, Cheras and Putrajaya 170, Seremban 189. Al Jazeera gives KL at about 170.
- National Haze Action Plan thresholds (DOE, Sep 2026): API above 100 stops outdoor activity. API above 150 for more than 24 h activates NADMA committees and allows cloud seeding. API above 200 closes schools (the King lowered this to 150 on 8 Oct). API above 300 forces industry to cut emissions. API above 500 allows a local emergency.
- Sources: "Schools, kindergartens to shut if API hits 200", NST, Sep 2026, https://www.nst.com.my/amp/news/nation/2026/09/1523596/schools-kindergartens-shut-if-api-hits-200 ; "Haze relief expected soon with shift in wind direction", The Star, 7 Oct 2026, https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/10/07/haze-relief-expected-soon-with-shift-in-wind-direction ; Malay Mail, 9 Oct 2026 (search summary), https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/10/09/haze-worsens-in-malaysia-nine-areas-breach-180-api-seremban-still-the-worst-at-189/238393

**Wind window.** MetMalaysia says the Southwest Monsoon, which began 14 May, ends and the
inter-monsoon runs from about 9 Oct to the end of October. That brings weak, variable winds and
afternoon thunderstorms [V] (The Star, 7 Oct). Earlier forecasts tied Klang Valley haze to
southwesterly winds and to hotspots in southern Sumatra and western Kalimantan [R] (IQAir, MetMalaysia
via search summary). For KL, the transport window from Sumatra is closing. Any deployment that starts
after mid-October helps KL less than it helps Sarawak.

## 2. Malaysia's firefighting assets

### 2.1 APMM (MMEA): the only Malaysian fixed-wing water bombers

- **2 x Bombardier CL-415MP**, ordered June 2008 (Malaysia was the Asian launch customer), delivered December 2008 and November 2009. Construction numbers 415-2068 and 415-2071. They carry side-looking radar, FLIR and a maritime surveillance suite, and are dual-role: patrol, SAR and water bombing. [V] Bombardier press release, https://bombardier.com/en/media/news/malaysian-maritime-enforcement-agency-buys-two-bombardier-415-multipurpose-aircraft ; aerialfiremag, 1 Mar 2024 (403; search summary), https://aerialfiremag.com/2024/03/01/water-bombers-of-the-malaysian-maritime-enforcement-agency/
- **Capacity:** 6,137 L per scoop, picked up in about 12 s at about 70 kt over a run of about 1,340 m of open water. [V] Katadata, https://katadata.co.id/digital/teknologi/6a8e7f7e7fba1/spesifikasi-bombardier-cl-415mp-malaysia-pesawat-amfibi-bantu-padamkan-karhutla
- **APMM air fleet:** 2 fixed-wing and 6 helicopters, including AS365N3 Dauphins [R].
- **Recent domestic use:** about 30,000 L on 60.7 ha of peat fire in Miri (2024) [R]. Betong and Sri Aman peat fires (2019) [R].
- **2015 Indonesia mission performance:** see section 3.3.

### 2.2 JBPM (Bomba), under the Ministry of Housing and Local Government (KPKT)

- **People:** about 15,000 (Dec 2024), 337 stations, with a gap of about 84 stations [V]. Volunteers: 8,421 in 369 teams during the 2024 monsoon floods [V]. "Gelombang kedua banjir...", Sinar Harian, https://www.sinarharian.com.my/ampArticle/701729 ; RTM, 2 Feb 2024, https://berita.rtm.gov.my/nasional/senarai-berita-nasional/senarai-artikel/bomba-bakal-miliki-10-helikopter-menjelang-2025/
- **Air Unit (Unit Udara):** bases in Subang, Miri and Kota Kinabalu [R]. Fleet history: 7 helicopters in 2016 (1 AW109, 2 AW139, 4 Mi-17) and 9 after 2 AW189s arrived in 2017-18 [V]. RTM (Feb 2024): target of 10 helicopters by 2025 [V]. TVS (Aug 2025): a concession deal brought the AW139 count to 6, with 4 on the peninsula and 1 new unit for Miri [R]. Plausible current fleet: about 6 AW139, 2 AW189 and 2 to 4 Mi-17 [A].
- **Buckets:** the Mi-17 carries a **1,590 L Bambi Bucket** (Bernama, Jan 2026, Pengerang peat fire) [V]. The AW139 bucket size is not published [A: about 1,000 to 1,200 L, the typical medium-twin Bambi size]. In an August 2025 Perak bushfire, an AW139 flew 20 drops; the crew reported power lines nearby and mud fouling the bucket at shallow dips [R] (Utusan, Aug 2025).
- **Peat technique:** the domestic norm is ground crews plus flooding drains and trenches to raise the water table, fed by JMG tube wells (85 wells built since 2009, RM23.7m) [V] (Borneo Post, 29 Mar 2019). Aerial support is used too. At Pengerang (Johor, 2026), 21 helicopter sorties dropped 33,000 L on a peat fire of nearly 100 ha, and a 17 ha peat fire needed "at least three days" with crews of 7 to 18 [V] (The Star, 7 and 9 Sep 2026; Star TV).
- **Bomba's own view of aircraft on peat (2015):** Director-General Wan Mohd Nor Ibrahim said water bombing in Indonesia was "not effective" because aircraft only spray from above, and that "we need to flood the area". He said Bomba was ready to send **1,500 personnel** to fight on the ground. [V] "Water-bombing is ineffective: Fire and Rescue Dept", NST, 13 Oct 2015, https://nst.com.my/amp/news/2015/10/105684/water-bombing-ineffective-fire-and-rescue-dept

### 2.3 TUDM (RMAF) and MINDEF

- **C-130H Hercules** (No. 20 Squadron, Subang) is the cloud-seeding workhorse. Each sortie carries about 800 kg of salt (4 tanks x 200 kg in 1,500 L of water each), released at 5,000 to 7,000 ft. By 30 Sep 2026, 14 operations and more than 50 flights had been flown nationwide [V]. NRES says Malaysia has "no dedicated cloud-seeding assets" and relying on TUDM is costly [V] (Bernama, 5 Oct 2026, https://www.bernama.com/en/general/news.php?id=2615818).
- **A400M** airlift. NADMA's SMART team flew 12.4 t of equipment to Nepal on an A400M in Sep 2026 [V]. This is the realistic lift for a ground team and its pumps.
- **AW139** (4 leased) and other utility helicopters [R]. No MAFFS-type retardant kit for the C-130 or A400M was found in any source; assume none exists [A].
- In 2015 Malaysia also sent a C-130 to Indonesia (for cloud seeding or transport) [V].

### 2.4 NADMA and SMART

- NADMA sits in the Prime Minister's Department and is the secretariat of the disaster committee chaired by DPM Zahid. In mid-Sep 2026, Zahid said NADMA would take over procurement of disaster-response assets for the police, fire service and civil defence [R].
- **SMART** (Special Malaysia Disaster Assistance and Rescue Team) is urban search and rescue, not wildland firefighting. Overseas deployments: Türkiye 2023 (70 personnel with Bomba and APM), Myanmar 2025, Nepal Sep 2026 (11 personnel). In 2005, 25 SMART personnel joined 100 firefighters in Riau [V]. "NADMA sends 11-member SMART team to Nepal", Malay Mail, 5 Sep 2026, https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/09/05/nadma-sends-11member-smart-team-to-nepal-for-flood-and-landslide-sar/234023

### 2.5 Competing domestic demand (decision-relevant)

Malaysia is fighting its own peat fires at the same time: Pengerang (Johor) in January and
September 2026, and Sarawak hotspots. MetMalaysia and NRES expect dry conditions to last to mid-2027
[V] (The Star, 6 Oct 2026, https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/10/06/bracing-for-a-hotter-drier-future).
Sending 1 of 2 CL-415MPs abroad leaves one at home. Sending both leaves none. This is why the offer
is one aircraft.

## 3. Past Malaysian help to Indonesia

### 3.1 1997-98: Operasi Jerebu (Operation Haze)

- Announced 23 Sep 1997 by Indonesia's Coordinating Minister Azwar Anas: **1,200 Malaysian firefighters to Sumatra**, about 200 fire engines on standby, and an offer of air force medical and communications experts and cloud seeding [V for the announcement]. "Concern Over Smoke Spreads Across S-E Asia", Korea Times, 23 Sep 1997, reproduced at https://weather.ou.edu/~spark/AMON/v1_n5/News/fire/smoke_SEAsia.html
- Bomba planned it at PM Mahathir's request. It lasted **25 days** in Sumatra and Kalimantan, with a documented site at Bayung Lincir, South Sumatra (Musi Banyuasin). Reported problems: lack of proper equipment, no water source, fire pits hidden in the peat. The monsoon rains, not suppression, ended the haze. [V] "Operation Haze", Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Haze ; NST, 9 Sep 2019, https://nst.com.my/amp/news/nation/2019/09/519986/malaysia-offers-help-indonesia-put-out-fires ; Siakapkeli (Bomba photo archive), https://siakapkeli.my/articles/24-tahun-berlalu-bomba-malaysia-bantu-negara-indonesia-padam-kebakaran-hutan-di-sumatera-selatan
- The exact headcount that actually deployed is not confirmed. 1,200 was announced. The figure "1,210" sometimes quoted could not be sourced [R].
- Context: hotspots peaked at about 37,938 in August 1997, and 8 to 10 Mha burned. Malaysia's losses for Aug to Oct 1997 were RM802m, including about **RM27m of extra government spending, the firefighters to Indonesia among it**. Sarawak was put under a state of emergency. [V] Wikipedia; "Haze: how much the problem costs us", Helena Varkkey, Between The Lines, https://betweenthelines.my/haze-how-much-the-problem-costs-us/

### 3.2 2005: Riau

- 15 Aug 2005: **100 firefighters, 25 SMART and 29 tonnes of equipment**, based at Pekanbaru, working in Riau and North Sumatra [V]. "Statement by the ASEAN Senior Officials on the Environment", ASEAN, 17 Aug 2005, https://asean.org/statement-by-the-asean-senior-officials-on-the-environment ; Reuters ScreenOcean, https://reuters.screenocean.com/record/376108
- Same fires, for comparison: Singapore SCDF Operation Lionheart sent 54 personnel to Rokan Hilir for 18 to 23 Aug. Over about 4.5 working days they covered "seven locations spanning over 10 ha". The fires were deep-seated [V]. https://www.scdf.gov.sg/home/about-scdf/international-engagement/overseas-missions/rokan-hilir-bush-fires-sumatra-indonesia
- **Implied ground productivity:** about 2 ha per day for a 54-person team, or about 0.04 ha per person-day on deep-seated fire. This is the best public number for sizing a foreign ground crew [A, derived].
- Follow-on: the Malaysia-Indonesia Riau programme (Rokan Hilir, with Global Environment Centre) built an air-quality station at Bagan Siapi-api, rehabilitated peat in 5 villages and built 10 check dams. It was completed by April 2010 [V]. ASEAN MSC media release, 30 Apr 2010, https://asean.org/media-release-of-the-ninth-meeting-of-the-sub-regional-ministerial-steering-committee-msc-on-transboundary-haze-pollution-balikpapan-east-kalimantan-indonesia-30-april-2010

### 3.3 2015: CL-415 to South Sumatra

- Indonesia **refused** Singapore's package on 12 Sep 2015. Minister Siti Nurbaya said Indonesia had "17 aircraft ready for water bombing and cloud seeding" and that one more water-bombing unit "would not make much difference". [V] "RI refuses Singapore's help in forest fires", Jakarta Post, 12 Sep 2015, https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2015/09/12/ri-refuses-singapore-s-help-forest-fires
- About 8 to 9 Oct, after weeks of refusals, Jokowi accepted help from Malaysia, Singapore, Australia, Russia, China and Japan. Indonesia said it wanted large aircraft (10,000 L or more). [V] "Indonesia accepts international help to combat fires", Inquirer/AFP, https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/729059/indonesia-accepts-international-help-to-combat-fires
- **Malaysian deployment:** 1 APMM CL-415MP, 1 AS365N3 Dauphin for survey, 1 TUDM C-130 and 25 APMM personnel. Departed 9 Oct, working from 11 Oct, scooping at Sungai Lumpur about 70 miles southeast of Palembang, about 7 minutes from scoop to fire. Results: **about 546,000 L in 5 days and 21 flight hours** (15 to 20 drops a day per Bernama, an average of 26 runs and 165,000 L a day per NST). Peak day 13 Oct: 78,000 L in 4 h 40 min. Aerialfiremag counts 176 drops and about 1 million L by 21 Oct. Aircraft and crew came home about 20 Oct for **roughly 100 hours of maintenance** before any redeployment. [V/R] "Malaysia to continue sending aircraft to 'waterbomb' Sumatera fires", NST, 20 Oct 2015, https://www.nst.com.my/amp/news/nation/2015/10/106766/malaysia-continue-sending-aircraft-waterbomb-sumatera-fires ; "MMEA aircraft flies 21 hours in 5 days...", Astro Awani/Bernama, https://de-awani-web-portal-stg.eco.astro.com.my/berita-malaysia/mmea-aircraft-flies-21-hours-5-days-fight-fires-indonesia-76582?amp=1 ; Katadata (above)
- **Singapore:** 1 RSAF Chinook with a 5,000 L bucket and 34 SAF personnel at Palembang, plus a C-130 for cloud seeding [V]. **Australia:** an L-100 Hercules air tanker [V]. **Russia:** Be-200s, per a Jakarta Post headline ("Indonesia brings Russian aircraft to tackle haze", 20 Oct 2015) whose text was not retrieved [R].
- **Indonesia's own effort (16 Oct 2015):** 32 aircraft (21 helicopters, 7 fixed-wing water bombers, 4 cloud seeding; 6 of the 32 foreign) and **22,146 ground personnel** in 6 provinces. Rp500bn of disaster funds over two months, South Sumatra the largest share. That day South Sumatra received 334 water drops. Hotspots that day: South Sumatra 537, Central Kalimantan 134, Jambi 97. BNPB spokesman Sutopo: "It is not easy to extinguish the massive hotspots, especially in the peatlands." [V] "Govt deploys 32 aircraft to tackle haze in biggest effort yet", Jakarta Post, 16 Oct 2015, https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2015/10/16/govt-deploys-32-aircrafts-tackle-haze-biggest-effort-yet

### 3.4 2019: offer declined

- 9 Sep 2019: Minister Yeo Bee Yin offers "any kind of assistance". Hotspots that day: Kalimantan 426, Sumatra 350. Indonesia had more than 9,000 personnel in the field and emergencies in 6 provinces [V] (NST, 9 Sep 2019, above).
- BNPB said Indonesia "did not yet require" foreign help. On 19 Sep Mahathir said, "I don't know why" Indonesia was not accepting Malaysia's water bombers [V]. "Mahathir: Ask Jokowi...", Mothership, Sep 2019, https://mothership.sg/2019/09/mahathir-jokowi-haze/ . An Indonesian embassy reply in Oct 2019 noted that Malaysia's Bombardier was itself in maintenance at the time [R] (DAP memorandum).
- Indonesia's effort: about 5,600 troops reinforced 9,000, reaching more than 29,000 by late September. **52 helicopters had dropped more than 263 million L, plus 164 t of salt** for cloud seeding [V]. Cloud seeding was hampered because the haze suppressed cloud formation [V]. Hotspots fell 78% over 26 to 28 Sep, **after rain** [V]. "Indonesia sends more people, aircraft...", Inquirer, https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1165528/indonesia-sends-more-people-aircraft-to-battle-forest-fires/amp ; "Officials rely on cloud seeding...", Jakarta Post, 2 Oct 2019, https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2019/10/02/officials-rely-on-cloud-seeding-to-combat-forest-fires
- Outcome: KLHK counted 1.64 Mha burned, 41% (670,000 ha) on peat. An independent Sentinel-2 estimate gives 3.11 Mha, 31% on peat [V]. The World Bank put damage and losses at **at least USD 5.2bn** (0.5% of GDP; USD 157m in direct damage and USD 5.0bn in lost activity) [V]. "World Bank says Indonesia fires cost $5.2 billion", CNBC/Reuters, 11 Dec 2019, https://www.cnbc.com/2019/12/11/world-bank-says-indonesia-fires-cost-5point2-billion-in-economic-losses.html

### 3.6 Pattern across episodes

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1997 | Firefighters, cloud seeding | Accepted | weeks | ~1,200 announced; 25 days on the ground |

Indonesia accepts when it is visibly overwhelmed (1997, late 2015, 2026). It accepts only on its own

province, and that a change of province needs fresh consent [A, resting on the 2015 and 2026 precedents
and on AATHP Art. 13].

## 4. What worked and what did not (evaluations)

- **Cost of inaction.** 2015: **USD 16.1bn**, 1.9 to 2% of GDP, 2.6 Mha burned. World Bank, "The Cost of Fire: An Economic Analysis of Indonesia's 2015 Fire Crisis", Feb 2016, https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/391e19f4-18f7-56c2-8c28-bb89cc9e7d6f [V]. Malaysia: RM802m (1997) and about RM1.5bn (2013), of which RM410m health in 2013 [V] (Varkkey, above).
- **Health.** Koplitz et al. 2016 (ERL 11:094023) estimate 100,300 excess deaths in Indonesia, Malaysia and Singapore from 2015 smoke, about 6,500 of them in Malaysia (95% CI 1,700 to 11,300). Malaysian population-weighted smoke exposure was about 14 µg/m³. https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/11/9/094023 [V]
- **Which provinces matter for Malaysia (most decision-relevant).** From Koplitz 2016 [V]:
  - **South Sumatra** caused more than 50% of regional smoke exposure in both 2006 and 2015, and 62% of the 2006-to-2015 rise in emissions.
  - **Jambi** roughly doubled its exposure share in 2015 and was the third-largest emitter.
  - **Central Kalimantan** emitted as much as South Sumatra in 2006 but caused only about a third of South Sumatra's exposure at each receptor.
  - **West Kalimantan** was the second-largest source of exposure in Malaysia in 2006. Malaysia here includes Sarawak, where West Kalimantan dominates.
  - **Riau** was not significant at any receptor in 2006 or 2015. This contradicts the 2013 June episode, which was Riau-driven [K]. Riau matters in June to August under strong southwesterlies.
  - Peat's share of fires rose from 2006 to 2015: Sumatra 44% to 72%, Kalimantan 32% to 43%.
- **Hotspot and land-type shares, 2015.** WRI: Central Kalimantan and South Sumatra each logged more than 27,000 alerts (the previous five-year average was about 5,500), with more than 60% on peat. 50% of South Sumatra's fires were in pulpwood concessions. Papua had 14,500 alerts. [V] https://www.wri.org/insights/exploring-indonesias-long-and-complicated-history-forest-fires . Greenpeace (more than 100,000 hotspots): Central Kalimantan 25%, South Sumatra 22%, about 10% in Papua, and 36% in pulp and oil palm concessions [R]. https://news.mongabay.com/2015/11/greenpeace-releases-dramatic-haze-photos-as-indonesian-fire-emissions-surpass-1-6b-tons/
- **Aerial water bombing on peat.** It works on surface flames and new fires. It does not put out smouldering peat. Evidence:
  - The Bomba DG in 2015 (above).
  - An Indonesian official estimate of about **500 t of water per hectare** of peat, which is why rain is the practical remedy [R] (Mongabay/Eco-Business, Mar 2021, https://news.mongabay.com/2021/03/peatland-fire-burning-season-starts-indonesia/).
  - A 2023 BALANGA review: fast and flexible, but costly, risky and less effective on peat [R].
  - Sutopo, 2015 (above). In 2015 and 2019 the fires ended when the rains came [V].
- **Cloud seeding.** It depends on suitable clouds and was blocked by haze in 2019 [V]. Malaysia gives it a 50 to 70% success rate at about RM100,000 per operation [V] (Bernama, 5 Oct 2026). Indonesian officials claim it is cheaper than water bombing for wetting peat [R].
- **Peat restoration (BRG, now BRGM).** About 1.6 Mha reported restored by 2024 [R]. Effectiveness studies:
  - Up to 22% fewer hotspots across nearly 4,000 km² where rewetting worked. Deep wells beat canal blocks on average [R].
  - Wooden canal blocks increased fires by up to 24% in one analysis. Concrete blocks did better [R] (EGU23-10738).
  - About 70% of canal blocks were damaged in a 2015-2024 study, and only blocks about a year old are reliably effective [R].
  - Castro et al. 2026 (iScience 29(6)): if all canal blocks worked, rewetting could avoid up to 6.4% of burnt area in West and Central Kalimantan [R].
  - Sources: https://www.conservationgateway.org/collections/land/effective-restoration-avoid-peatland-fires-kalimantan-indonesia/ ; "Indonesia's peatlands show restoration can work", Jakarta Post/The Star, 19 Aug 2026, https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/08/19/comment-indonesias-peatlands-show-restoration-can-work
- **Institutions on the ground.**
  - Manggala Agni (forestry fire brigades) had about 2,100 personnel on standby in 2017. South Sumatra had 303 across 4 Daops (Banyuasin, OKI, Musi Banyuasin, Lahat) plus 669 in support (2018) [R].
  - Masyarakat Peduli Api (community fire groups): South Sumatra had 64 squads with 960 members (2018) [R].
  - These local units, and Satgas Karhutla (TNI, Polri and BPBD task forces, at 54,394 personnel in 2026), are who any Malaysian unit works alongside.
- **Enforcement.** Arrests and licence suspensions recur every year: 2019 (230 arrests, 30 companies sealed), 2026 (162 suspects, 95 companies). None of it changes this season's fires. It is prevention for the next season.

**Net lesson for targeting.** Foreign aircraft can make a difference on new, surface or mineral-soil
fires near a water source and outside dense smoke, if they arrive early. They cannot put out
established peat fires. Ground crews can mop up a few hectares a day of deep-seated fire. The marginal
haze benefit of a small force is highest when it is aimed at:

1. fires in provinces upwind of the receptor (for KL, South Sumatra and Jambi, then West Kalimantan);
2. fires early in their life, before they become deep peat burns;
3. sites within a short flight of a scoopable water body.

## 5. Institutions and politics

### 5.1 ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution (AATHP)

Signed 10 Jun 2002, in force 25 Nov 2003. Indonesia ratified last, by Law No. 26/2014 on
16 Sep 2014, and deposited its instrument in early 2015 [V]. Tempo, https://en.tempo.co/amp/607678/indonesia-ratifies-agreement-on-trans-boundary-haze-pollution .
Key text, read from the ASEAN treaty PDF (https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/ASEANAgreementonTransboundaryHazePollution-1.pdf) [V]:

- **Art. 3(1):** each party has a sovereign right to exploit its own resources and a responsibility not to harm other states.
- **Art. 5(2):** the ASEAN Centre "shall work on the basis that the national authority will act first to put out the fires". Only after declaring an emergency "may" a party request assistance through the Centre.
- **Art. 12(2):** "Assistance can only be employed at the request of and with the consent of the requesting Party, or, when offered..., with the consent of the receiving Party." **Art. 12(5):** the requesting party specifies the scope and type of assistance.
- **Art. 13(1):** the receiving party "shall exercise the overall direction, control, co-ordination and supervision of the assistance within its territory". The assisting party keeps immediate operational supervision of its own personnel and equipment. **Art. 13(2):** the host provides local facilities and protects the assisting party's personnel and equipment.
- **Art. 14:** the host gives the assisting party's personnel exemptions and facilities, waives duties on equipment, and eases entry and exit.
- The treaty says nothing about who pays for assistance. Art. 12(6) only asks parties to notify "the terms, especially financial" on which help could be given. Assume the assisting state pays its own costs [A, consistent with 2015 and 2026 practice].

**Product implication.** Indonesia chooses the area and directs the work. The Malaysian commander
controls only how his crews and aircraft operate. A recommendation such as "send them to South Sumatra"
is advice for a bilateral request. It is not something Malaysia can order.

### 5.2 ACC THPC

The AATHP Art. 5 Centre. Indonesia's offer to host it was welcomed at COP-1 in 2004 and endorsed again
in 2015. Its **secretariat office was inaugurated in April 2026 in East Jakarta** by Environment
Minister Hanif Faisol Nurofiq [R]. A Pekanbaru location, often cited, was **not confirmed** in this
research. It is too new to coordinate operations this season [A]. Indonesia Business Post,
https://indonesiabusinesspost.com/6534/asean/indonesia-launches-asean-haze-control-centre-to-curb-cross-border-pollution

### 5.3 ASMC (Singapore)

ASMC runs alert levels 0 to 3 for southern ASEAN. Level 1 is the dry season. Level 2 means rising risk:
haze, dry weather, and winds toward neighbours. Level 3 means high hotspot counts on two consecutive
days with dense plumes. Only high-confidence VIIRS hotspots are reported at ASEAN meetings [V].
https://asmc.asean.org/asmc-alerts/ . In 2026: Level 2 on 3 Aug, Level 3 on 26 Aug [V].

### 5.4 Indonesian side: who decides and how

- **President** (Prabowo) gives political consent, as on 18 Sep 2026.
- **Kemlu** (Foreign Affairs) handles the formal note and procedures. Malaysia said on 7 Oct that procedures were still being aligned [V].
- **BNPB** runs the air fleet and emergency operations under provincial emergency status, and coordinated Japan's Chinooks with TNI and Polri [V].
- **Kemenhut** (Forestry): SiPongi, Manggala Agni, the six-province priority list [V].
- **KLH** (Environment): AATHP focal point, enforcement, ACC THPC host. Kompas names the minister who received Kurup's letter as Mohammad Jumhur Hidayat. April 2026 reports name Hanif Faisol Nurofiq. Possibly a reshuffle; unresolved [R].
- **Provincial governments and BPBDs** declare the emergency statuses that let BNPB deploy.
- **Legal channel:** PP 23/2008 and Perka BNPB 22/2010 cover international organisations and foreign NGOs, **not foreign governments**. Government-to-government help therefore runs through diplomacy (Kemlu) and the AATHP, case by case [V]. https://www.rcrc-resilience-southeastasia.org/disaster-law/disaster-law-mapping-asean-agreement-on-disaster-management-and-emergency-response/country-profiles/indonesia
- **Sovereignty record:**
  - 2015: VP Jusuf Kalla said neighbours had enjoyed "11 months" of clean air without thanks [V] (Mothership, Oct 2015). Concession maps were called "classified information" [V] (Jakarta Post, 12 Sep 2015).
  - 2019: help declined [V]. 2023: haze denied [V].
  - 2026: Prabowo accepted, but limited help to Kalimantan, and the paperwork has lagged three weeks [V].
  - Academic framing: Varkkey, "Indonesian perspectives on managing the ASEAN haze", SARJANA, https://jml.um.edu.my/index.php/SARJANA/article/view/10352 [R].

### 7.3 Rules the ranking should encode

1. **Upwind of KL first.** South Sumatra, Jambi and Riau feed KL under southwesterlies; West and Central Kalimantan feed Sarawak and, more weakly, the peninsula (Koplitz 2016 [V]). In the 2026 inter-monsoon, recompute from trajectories: winds are weak and variable from about 9 Oct [V].
2. **Respect the mandate.** Default the allowed area to Kalimantan. Show a separate "if Indonesia agrees" ranking for Sumatra, because the KL benefit there is likely larger.
3. **Young, surface and mineral-soil fires near water outrank old deep-peat fires** for aircraft. Ground crews go to the highest-impact smouldering peat within reach of water.
4. **Flyability.** Exclude targets under dense plume or with visibility under 3 km. Prefer targets within about 7 minutes' flight of a scoopable river or lake for the CL-415. Helicopters can use smaller water bodies.
5. **Time-box.** Assume 10 aircraft-days per CL-415 rotation, and a 3 to 4 week political lag between offer and work (2015 and 2026 [V]).
6. **Weather beats suppression.** In 1997, 2015 and 2019 the rains ended the crisis [V]. Show the expected rain date and value suppression only for the days before it.
