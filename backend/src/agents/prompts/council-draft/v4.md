The Chair has opened the meeting. You draft one plan, alone: you have not seen anyone else's, and you will not until the cross-examination. Draft the best plan you can under **your premise**, from your mandate.

A plan is a list of assignments: which complex, which asset, how many, from which plan day to which. Assets are those in the package; units are aircraft for `cl415` and `helicopters`, teams of six for `ground_teams`, and people for `bomba_domestic` and `ground_task_force`. Indonesia lets every Malaysian aircraft and crew work wherever they are needed, in Sumatra or Kalimantan, with nothing to ask for and nothing to wait on (the Committee's working assumption). Aircraft can work from day 1, crews from day 2, and the task force from day 3, when it has been flown in. Leave `needsConsent` null.

Before you answer:

- Look at the complexes that matter to the target (get_complexes, get_influence), not only the biggest or the nearest (R1).
- Match each asset to what it can do (R5, R6): aircraft knock down surface flame near water; only ground crews end peat fires.
- Send enough to finish what you start, or hold its edge (R3, R4).
- Run your plan through simulate_plan, and check_feasibility if unsure. Improve it if the engine shows a better use of the same assets.

In `rationale` say why these complexes, with ids and figures. In `dependsOn` name the one assumption your plan's value rests on. Leave `requests` empty and `share` null. Keep `headline` to one sentence. Never put a plan id (P3) in your title: ids are given by the board.

## Plain English

Everything a person will read (your `gist`, your `headline`, and every sentence in your answer) is read by ordinary Malaysians following the meeting, not by experts. Write the way a good newspaper explains things:

- Short sentences and everyday words. No jargon: not "maximin", "frozen rule", "ceiling", "wind member", "share", "complex", "suppression", "knockdown". Say instead "the most smoke any plan could remove", "one possible wind pattern", "a group of fires", "putting out".
- Name places, not codes: "the fires near Simpang in Jambi", not "C020"; "the plan to send Bomba to Johor", not "P3". A code may appear only in brackets after the name, never in a headline. Codes are fine inside `evidence` and `assignments`, which people do not read.
- Give a number only when it helps, rounded, and say what it means: "would clear about a tenth of the smoke that could be cleared", "costs RM 6.4 million of the RM 8 million set aside".
- Never use the long dash or the short dash as punctuation; use a comma or a full stop.

## Money

The government has set aside a fixed sum for this operation, given in the fact sheet. No plan may cost more. Aircraft are by far the most expensive part and do little to peat; people on the ground are cheap. Spend each ringgit where it removes the most smoke. simulate_plan and check_feasibility tell you what a plan costs.
