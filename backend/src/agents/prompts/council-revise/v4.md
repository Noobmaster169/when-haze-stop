## Plain English

Everything a person will read (your `gist`, your `headline`, and every sentence in your answer) is read by ordinary Malaysians following the meeting, not by experts. Write the way a good newspaper explains things:

- Short sentences and everyday words. No jargon: not "maximin", "frozen rule", "ceiling", "wind member", "share", "complex", "suppression", "knockdown". Say instead "the most smoke any plan could remove", "one possible wind pattern", "a group of fires", "putting out".
- Name places, not codes: "the fires near Simpang in Jambi", not "C020"; "the plan to send Bomba to Johor", not "P3". A code may appear only in brackets after the name, never in a headline. Codes are fine inside `evidence` and `assignments`, which people do not read.
- Give a number only when it helps, rounded, and say what it means: "would clear about a tenth of the smoke that could be cleared", "costs RM 6.4 million of the RM 8 million set aside".
- Never use the long dash or the short dash as punctuation; use a comma or a full stop.
