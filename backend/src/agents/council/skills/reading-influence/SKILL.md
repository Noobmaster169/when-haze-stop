How to read where the target's smoke comes from.

## What a share means

A complex's share is the part of the ceiling it causes: the target's mean PM2.5 over the horizon from smoke not yet emitted. Shares add up. Putting a complex fully out from day 1 removes its whole share; putting half of it out removes half. A share is the engine's, from this week's fires and the wind members' wind; it is not a fixed fact about a province.

## Why the biggest or nearest fire is not the cause

Smoke reaches a place only if the wind carries it there before rain and time remove it. In the week of 10 October 2026 the ten complexes with most influence on KL caused 85% of its coming fire smoke; the ten with most fire power 49%; the ten nearest 5%. Two small fires in Johor caused more than whole Kalimantan complexes. Rank by share, then look at size and distance only to judge effort and access.

## What the literature says about KL

In the south-west monsoon (June to September), central Sumatra (Riau and northern Jambi) gives about 70% of the fire PM2.5 in Kuala Lumpur, southern Sumatra 17 to 20% and only in August to October (Reddington et al. 2014). For September 2019, Riau and Jambi gave about 97% of fire PM2.5 at Greater KL stations: Riau dominated Klang, Kuala Selangor and Bangi; Jambi dominated Petaling Jaya, Cheras, Batu Muda and Putrajaya (Murulitharan et al., EGU 2024). For Singapore it is the reverse: southern Sumatra leads. Kalimantan drives Kuching and western Sarawak. Per unit emitted, Sumatran fires harm the region more than Kalimantan's. Treat this as the prior; the engine's shares for this week are the evidence.

## Wind members

Past the forecast, each member replays a past window of wind. They are possible futures, not a forecast. A plan whose value rests on one member is fragile (R9). MetMalaysia declared the inter-monsoon from 9 October 2026: weak, variable winds and afternoon storms, so rain scrubs smoke and the Sumatra-to-KL transport window narrows.

## Timing

Smoke from Riau reaches the Malaysian west coast in 17 to 48 hours; from Kalimantan two to four days of steady east-south-easterly wind. Suppression takes days to bite. A benefit that arrives after the horizon, or after the rains, is worth nothing to this decision.

## Caveats to state

Models underestimate fire PM2.5, so absolute ug/m3 are uncertain while rankings hold up better. Satellites miss smouldering peat under cloud and smoke. Stations 30 km apart in the Klang Valley get different source mixes.
