What Malaysia's aircraft can and cannot do.

## The CL-415MP

APMM's Bombardier CL-415MP scoops 6,137 L from open water in a 1.3 km run and drops it, 6 to 10 drops an hour when the water is within about 10 nautical miles. In October 2015, at Indonesia's request, it flew from Palembang with a TUDM C-130 for logistics: about 546,000 L in 5 days and 21 flight hours, 26 runs and about 165,000 L a day on its best days, then home after about ten days for roughly 100 hours of maintenance. Plan 100,000 to 160,000 L a day for 10 operating days. It needs open water or a long, wide river within about 25 km; the fact sheet's sea distance does not map rivers.

## Helicopters

Three medium helicopters with buckets of 1,200 to 1,590 L (a Malaysian Mi-17's bucket is 1,590 L), about 20 drops each a day near water: 70,000 to 95,000 L a day for three.

## Where they help

On flaming surface fires on mineral soil, near water, a scooper knocks down several to about 15 ha of fire front a day. On deep peat the same water saturates well under 2 ha, and the whole Malaysian air package over its deployment would saturate 4 to 6 ha (R5). Use aircraft for first strikes on new ignitions, for flame on top of peat, and to protect ground crews, not to extinguish peat.

## What grounds them

Smoke: Japan's Chinooks were grounded in West Kalimantan in 2026 whenever visibility fell below about 3 km. Crew rest, maintenance cycles, and overflight and basing clearances. Cloud seeding needs cloud; in 2019 the haze itself blocked it.

## Costs

The CL-415 costs about EUR 15,000 a flight hour. Indonesia paid Rp 200 to 300 million a helicopter flight hour in Riau in 2019, when helicopters took about 70% of its fire budget.
