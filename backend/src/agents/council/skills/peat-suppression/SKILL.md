What puts out a tropical peat fire, and what does not.

## How peat burns

Peat smoulders at 500 to 700 C, against 1,000 to 1,500 C for flame. It ignites easily, keeps burning down to 10% oxygen, spreads slowly (about 0.1 to 1.5 m a day) and burns down as well as out, on average 0.45 m deep in the tropics and up to 3 m. Once groundwater drops more than about 40 cm below the surface the risk climbs steeply. Peat fires last weeks to months and usually end only with heavy rain. A smouldering front can travel underground and show little until it nears the surface.

## Why peat matters most for haze

Per kg burned, peat gives 3 to 6 times the particulate of other fires: 17 to 22 g of PM2.5 per kg for pure smouldering peat, more when flame burns on top. In 2015, 95% of Indonesia's fire PM2.5 came from peatland. Fire power measures flame and badly under-counts smouldering peat (R2). A complex with a high night share and many days of burning is deep smouldering peat.

## What works

- Ground crews with pumps and lances are the only reliable way to extinguish smouldering peat: push the lance in until the peat is slurry, dig out remnants, come back and probe again. Field reports give 0.25 to 3 ha a team-day; Singapore's crew in Riau in 2005 managed about 0.04 ha a person-day on deep peat. Water must be within a few hundred metres.
- Peat needs 3.8 to 6.4 L of water per kg: hundreds of thousands to millions of litres per hectare of deep burning.
- Aircraft knock down surface flame. They do not reach the burning layer. South Sumatra flew 6,826 sorties in two months of 2015 and fires still relit. Malaysia's own Bomba chief said in 2015 that aircraft cannot put out peat.
- Canal blocking and cloud seeding are seasonal levers, not tools to aim at one fire.

## Re-ignition

Peat relights if left above about 50 to 60 C. Half-suppressed peat relights; unsuppressed fire next door burns back into treated, still-dry ground (R3, R4). Count a fire out only after probing or several dry nights without a detection, and keep capacity in reserve.

## Rules that follow

Prefer new, small fires on peat that crews can reach: caught early they are small, left they become the months-long fires. Send only what can finish the job or hold the edge advancing into unburned peat; leave the interior of a large established fire to the rain. Attack a cluster from its edge and as a whole, never its middle. Never plan aircraft to extinguish peat.
